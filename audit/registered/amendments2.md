## Amendment 2 (1 Nov 2026, before any price download)

No price data has been received and no result has been examined. This amendment changes no entry rule, threshold, sample size, period, cost or decision criterion. It adds reporting rules and records the outcome of the bootstrap verification.

| # | Item | What is recorded |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Universe interpretation** | Every result table is labelled by what it can support. *General strategy evidence*: results from the 620-stock random sample of today's listed common stocks. This is not a reconstruction of ApexScan's historical universe, which was index members (S&P 500, NASDAQ-100, S&P 400, Russell 2000) and cannot be rebuilt because historical membership is unavailable. *Evidence directly applicable to ApexScan*: only Population A (Test B, what the live app actually produced) and, as a proxy only, the secondary liquid subset (price at least $5, median dollar volume at least $2M). The proxy is never described as the ApexScan universe. The final report carries both labels in its conclusions |
| 2 | **Population A maturity** | A discovery is *matured* at horizon H only if the open H sessions after its entry open exists in the data. Entry is the open of the first session after the scan date. Data end on 30 Oct 2026. Counts known now: 895 discoveries; 5 have no entry session yet; at the registered primary horizon of 63 sessions, **0 are matured**; at 21 sessions, 851 are matured and 44 are not; at 126 sessions, 0 are matured. Of the 890 with an entry, 39 have 1 to 20 sessions of history, 33 have 21 to 41 and 818 have 42 to 62. The earliest discovery reaches 63 sessions in early November 2026. Matured and immature observations are reported in separate tables. Returns measured to the latest close for immature observations are shown by age band and are never pooled with, or averaged against, matured returns. No statement about the 63-session primary horizon is made for Population A until observations have matured |
| 3 | **Bootstrap verification** | The registered p-value is the percentile-inversion p-value of a bootstrap that resamples tickers as clusters: p = 2 x min(share of bootstrap differences at or below 0, share at or above 0). Verification: (a) 14,400 null simulations across 18 designs (150, 300 and 600 tickers; ticker effect 0 and 0.08; normal, skewed and heavy-tailed returns): rejection rate at a nominal 5% was 5.1% for the registered method, 5.0% for a null-imposed (re-centred) bootstrap, 5.1% for statsmodels cluster-robust regression; Monte Carlo error about 0.2 points. At 1% the rates were 0.8%, 0.9% and 1.0%. (b) A second null design where each arm has its own ticker effect and trades repeat within a ticker (4,000 simulations): registered 4.5% to 5.9%, null-imposed 4.4% to 5.9%, statsmodels cluster-robust 4.2% to 5.7%, while a test that ignores clusters rejected 14.9% to 17.3%. This confirms that clustering by ticker is needed and that the registered p-value is not inflated. (c) The 95% interval matches scipy.stats.bootstrap (percentile, paired clusters) within 0.4 points. (d) The p-value matches the null-imposed p-value within 0.03 and statsmodels within a median of less than 0.02. (e) Breaking the code (one-sided p, no doubling, shifted distribution, non-clustered resampling) is caught by the tests. **Conclusion: no change to the p-value method is required.** Holm correction across E2, E3 and E4 is retained. Caveat: an earlier, smaller run (300 simulations per design) showed 7% to 10% in a few skewed or heavy-tailed designs, and all four methods moved together, so it reflects those simulated datasets rather than the bootstrap. The larger repeat with different random draws did not reproduce it (every design between 3.9% and 6.2%). Heavy-tailed returns can still make a single sample's p-value less reliable, which is a further reason for the Amendment 1 minimum sample sizes |
| 4 | **Cost sensitivity** | Every comparison is reported at half cost (0.5x), registered cost (1x) and double cost (2x), computed from the stored gross entry and exit prices. Each row carries a `cost_dependent` flag, true when the evidence class, the Holm significance or the sign of the difference changes across the three levels. Any conclusion that rests on the cost assumption is named as such in the final report. The decision rule is evaluated at registered cost, unchanged. A variant supported at 1x but not at 2x is reported as *supported, fragile to cost*, never as unconditionally supported |
